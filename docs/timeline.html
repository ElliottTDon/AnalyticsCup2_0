Timeline and next steps

Football defensive positioning. How can tracking and contextual data help us understand how players and teams defend? Team shape, spacing, pressure, compactness, marking, defensive decision-making.

20 matches in total. Pose is available on two. Body pose was not used for full-back tracking, overlaps, or centre-backs drawn out.

Order

  1. The mission was how tracking explains defending.
  2. Event EDA on 20 A-League matches showed block height and on-ball pressure already separate shots.
  3. A coupled two-graph method asked whether the defense covers the passing graph or only the ball, and mostly lost to the box and pressure.
  4. Seven sharper hypotheses were split out.
  5. Three are finished: full-back tracking, overlaps, and centre-backs drawn out.
  6. Four have since finished: body shape, sight of goal, shadow screening, and the wide-to-midfield passing angle.

What those four found

  • Body shape: defenders are usually square to the receiver. Two matches only. Do not ship it as a KPI yet.
  • Sight of goal: at least half the goal mouth is supported on two matches. A film label, not a league KPI yet.
  • Shadow screening: keep a lane-occupation flag on high-block passing options, because forward completion drops. Do not claim it lowers pass value.
  • Passing angle: a larger angle is less likely to break the first line.

Next steps

  • Keep nearest-defender distance on overlaps, and the full-back tracking rule, as reception metrics, not shot models.
  • Film centre-backs when the striker is inside 3 m.
  • Do not ship coupled-graph scores as a KPI.
  • Do not ship the body-shape result as a KPI yet.

The earlier review already had three coaching levers from the event labels: stay organised, keep the ball outside the shape, and manage low-block width.
