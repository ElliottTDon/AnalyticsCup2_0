What the numbers are

There is no xG, xGOT, or xT in this data. The numbers use shot rate, reception, xthreat, and distance.

1. Full-back tracking

A full back on an overlap or underlap is tracked only when the nearest outfield opponent is within 6 m, goal-side, and moving with them for 1 second (direction cosine at least 0.60, speed difference at most 2 m/s).

260 attacking-half runs, 50 tracked, 210 not, 17 matches with both. About 19% of these runs are fully tracked.

Tracked runs were received 12.9 points less often (20.0% vs 32.9%, interval −0.250 to −0.015).

Shots (16.0% vs 17.6%, interval −0.109 to +0.078) and xthreat (0.0159 vs 0.0194, interval −0.0076 to +0.0004) do not separate.

Worth keeping as a reception metric, not a shot metric.

2. Overlaps and underlaps

534 runs in 20 matches (301 overlaps, 233 underlaps). 17.2% lead to a shot.

Runs that do not lead to a shot have a defender 1.33 m closer (4.84 m vs 6.17 m, interval −2.31 to −0.47).

Goal-side position (56.9% of runs) and a defender in the lane (30.0%) do not separate shots, xthreat, or being beaten by the run. Beaten-by-movement is rare (11 runs).

This is an association, not a prevention system. The useful metric is nearest-defender distance at the run, not lane or body-side.

3. Defenders drawn out

Tight mark = nearest opponent within 3 m of the striker. Drawn out = metres from that role's own median spot in the same block.

1,171 striker possessions in organized blocks, tight on 45.6%.

  • Centre-backs: +1.60 m (interval 0.49 to 2.67), supported.
  • Central midfielders: +1.71 m (interval −0.02 to 3.62), not supported, and only 445 possessions.
  • Full backs: +1.02 m (interval −0.21 to 2.26), not supported.

Tight marks also come with more shots: +8.5 points (22.5% vs 14.0%, interval 0.042 to 0.129). xthreat and line breaks do not move.

A goal-side assignment at the same 3 m cutoff does not clear zero for any role.

Already on the books

In organized blocks, shot rate is about 19% low, 9% medium, and 6% high. On-ball pressure runs from 2.2% shots with none to 21.4% with very high pressure. Coupled formation graphs did not add a useful split after the box and the pressure on the ball.

Mission

Football defensive positioning. How can tracking and contextual data help us understand how players and teams defend? Team shape, spacing, pressure, compactness, marking, defensive decision-making.

What was tested

Twenty A-League 2024/25 matches (SkillCorner Open Data). Phases of play + dynamic events (pressure, on-ball engagements, organised-defense / inside-shape labels, pressing chains). Plus an earlier graph experiment: defense proximity + attack Delaunay, Hungarian marking, supra-Laplacian — compared to OOP width/length/area, nearest defender, and on-ball pressure.

Findings (visual EDA)

Earlier page detail. The short paragraph above is the established block and pressure result. This list is not a new study.

  • Shot rate by organized block: low ~18.5%, medium ~9.0%, high ~6.0%.
  • Shot phases sit in a smaller defensive box (strongest in the low block, ~150–166 m² smaller).
  • On-ball pressure ladder: ~2.2% (none) → ~21% (very high) — but high bands often mean the ball is already dangerous.
  • Organised defense ~2.5% shot rate vs ~10.5% when not; ball inside shape ~11.5% vs ~5.8% outside.
  • Engagement types: pressing / counter-press regain more; beaten-by-possession or movement flags precede shots.
  • Team board: mean OOP box area alone does not rank shot prevention (e.g. Melbourne City large box, low shot rate).

Findings (graph notebook)

  • After residualizing on width, length, area, nearest defender, and overall pressure, most graph features do not add separation. Only supra-Laplacian λ₂ keeps a small residual (about −0.013).
  • Uncovered danger, free attackers, defense λ₂, structural pressure, and a late λ₂ drop do not beat the box+pressure baseline.

How to read that

Contextual labels already carry most of the shot signal: block height, box compactness, organised shape, and whether the ball is inside the block. Graph coupling did not reveal a large extra effect in this sample. Twenty matches is still small.

Brainstorm

  • Compactness vs decision If the bounding box already measures compactness, the remaining question is who steps, who holds, and when the block slides versus jumps.
  • Pressure as context, not cause Very high pressure co-occurs with shots. Film the subset where pressure is high but the box is open — those are the decision mistakes, not the average ladder.
  • Marking intent versus proximity Body pose exists for two matches only. Beaten-by-movement engagements are the practical proxy until pose coverage grows.
  • Box–coupling disagreements Cases where the box looks compact but supra-Laplacian coupling is weak: those disagreements are the film to watch.
  • Monday coaching change Actionable levers so far: stay organised, keep the ball outside the shape, manage low-block width. If that is all a coach would change, we still need a sharper decision metric.
