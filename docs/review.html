Defensive positioning review

Review the defensive question and note ideas on the go.

Mission

Football defensive positioning. How can tracking and contextual data help us understand how players and teams defend? Team shape, spacing, pressure, compactness, marking, defensive decision-making.

What was tested

Twenty A-League 2024/25 matches (SkillCorner). Organized blocks only (low, medium, high). Two graphs per frame: a defense proximity graph and an attack Delaunay graph, coupled by Hungarian marking and a supra-Laplacian. Compared against the trivial baselines already in the data: out-of-possession width, length, area, nearest defender to the ball, and on-ball pressure.

Findings

  • Shot rate by block: low about 18.5–18.7%, medium about 9.0–9.3%, high about 6.0%.
  • On-ball pressure separates shots: no pressure 2.2% to very high pressure 21.4%.
  • Shot phases have a smaller defensive box, strongest in the low block (about 148–166 m² smaller).
  • After residualizing on width, length, area, nearest defender, and overall pressure, graph features mostly do not add separation. Only supra-Laplacian λ₂ still has a residual shot-minus-nonshot difference of −0.013 (bootstrap interval −0.022 to −0.004). Uncovered danger, free attackers, defense λ₂, structural pressure, and a λ₂ drop in the last 2 seconds do not.

How to read that

The block’s box and the pressure on the ball-carrier already carry the shot signal in this sample. The interaction of the two graphs did not reveal a large extra effect. Twenty matches is a small sample.

Brainstorm

  • Compactness vs decision If the bounding box already measures compactness, the remaining question is a decision: who steps, who holds the line, and when the block slides versus jumps.
  • Marking intent versus proximity Body pose (shoulder heading) exists for two matches only; proximity matching did not find free attackers that predict shots. Intent may matter more than distance, but we barely have the signal to test that.
  • Box–coupling disagreements Cases where the box looks compact but the supra-Laplacian coupling is weak: those disagreements are the film to watch, not the average.
  • Ball pressure vs covering the graph Pressing the ball-carrier (the pressure label) versus covering the rest of the passing graph. The second did not add much here. Ask whether that is because the sample is organized blocks, where the ball-carrier pressure and the box move together.
  • Monday coaching change What a coach would change on Monday if the only reliable split is block height and box area. If the answer is thin, the metric is not yet actionable enough.
